- **Margot Pell, the conservator.** A restorer from the Zuzu City museum, sent for when you show Gunther your first damaged old painting (she arrives a week after his letter), and she's a second romance (marriageable by any farmer). She starts in a room down the steps from Gus's storeroom at the Saloon and works at the museum. Six heart events, from a painting with a gate missing to both your names on a museum card. At 4 hearts Robin builds her a cottage by the river, with a big north window, a workroom and a little kitchen (you choose what's planted in its window box), and her Saloon room becomes Gus's guest room. Married, she has her own corner of the farmhouse, an easel and a rack of her own small canvases on the patio, her own wedding, and her own lines.
- **Julian and Margot.** On one of his visit days, if you've met them both, they meet at the Saloon and argue about whether restoring a painting is art, and you settle it. After that they keep bickering on his visits, a little fonder each time.
- **Painting Mastery and restoration.** Past Painting level 10, a mastery bar fills as you paint, and Margot sends you her old Study Board to hang up: it fills with pinned studies as the bar does, she remarks on each quarter, and she posts another if yours goes missing. When it's full, Margot sets you a test restoration at the museum, in the mode you choose (Zuzu at Dusk, lent by the city's museum for the purpose); restore it well and you're a conservator: the Master's Easel (more light, and gold-or-better paintings made at it are living masterworks that move when hung: in a scene, water glints, firelight flickers and leaves stir; across anything else, window light drifts), and the right to restore old paintings yourself. Before then, Margot restores them for you, for a fee, and posts each one back to you three days later, attached to her letter.
- **Old paintings.** Twenty-one damaged old paintings in five sets turn up around the valley: in artifact spots (the Old Valley), fishing treasure (the Wreck), mine crates (the Miners), the Skull Cavern (Calico) and Volcano chests (the Dwarves), and now and then at the Travelling Cart. The first is guaranteed from Painting level 3 (and replaced if you lose it before Gunther sees it), and a painting you lose or sell comes back as readily as a new one until the museum has it. Restore one at the easel and it's worth far more than your own work.
- **The museum's collection.** Choose **Donate a restored painting** at Gunther's counter (framed or not: he gives the frame back) and it joins the museum's collection. The collection (at his counter) and the wing's cards describe every painting, found or not, and say where to look for the missing ones. Each set finished brings its own frame style (the Old Valley brings a velvet rope, a gallery bench and a marble plinth to set something on; Sable sells more plinths from Painting 6); six bring a living painting whose water shimmers; twelve bring Margot's loupe, worn like a ring, so old paintings turn up more often; all 21 bring a paint-tube Junimo from Sable. Gunther tells you when a donation has earned something, and it comes in the next morning's post.
- **The Painting Wing.** After your first donation, a new wing opens off the museum's exhibit hall: the Old Valley framed along the back wall, and every other painting on its own white marble display, with a place kept for each one still missing. Margot visits on Tuesday and Thursday afternoons.
- **Frames that do something.** Sixteen frame styles, from pine and oak to prismatic and Qi's, each with a rarity and a trait. A framed painting hung in a room helps paintings made at an easel there: more light, sparks sooner, paint saved, more XP, or more value (the three strongest frames in a room count). Sable sells the common ones, and more as your Painting level rises; the rarest come from the Night Market, the museum, the finished portfolio and Mr. Qi. The framing screen shows every frame you have, and what it does.
- **The Framer's Bench.** Takes a framed painting out of its frame overnight, so frames can be collected and swapped. Works with Automate.
- **The portfolio, finished.** A sixth page, Faces of the Valley (portraits of the town's groups and visitors). Finishing every page brings the gold-leaf frame, and then painting the view from the Summit brings Sable to the top with you to look through the book.
- **Julian buys a painting.** Once a season, on a visit day, he'll buy one of your paintings at twice the shop's price, and tell you what he thinks of it. He also has a winter coat now, for his winter visits.
- **Animals.** Paint your pet, your horse and the farm animals you own, from any easel. The record has an Animals tab.
- **Festivals.** An easel at Spirit's Eve, in the heart of the haunted maze, and on the beach at the Dance of the Moonlight Jellies, each with a view you can only paint that night. If Sable is your secret friend at the Feast of the Winter Star, their gift is a painting.
- **Special orders.** Lewis wants three portraits for the town hall, Gus two paintings for the Saloon's walls, Leah a view of the Secret Woods, Elliott the beach at night, and Mr. Qi a Masterpiece of a legendary fish.
- **Relaxed is Normal with no clock, and there's a new Simplified mode.** Relaxed used to leave out glazes and highlights, so a grey ghost came out green and brown. Now Relaxed only takes away the time limit: no light meter, and sparks wait for you, but the picture is Normal's, paint comes from your Palette, and mixed colours dry. Everything Relaxed used to make easy is the new **Simplified** mode: free paint that never dries, a wider mixing band, and plain-paint pictures with no glazes or highlights (a config switch keeps them, for free paint with the whole picture). **If you play on Relaxed, you'll now need a Palette and paint**, or switch to Simplified on the easel. Thanks to Hiname for the feedback.
- **The light fits the painting.** How long the light lasts now comes from how much there is to paint (each colour area's edge, each fill, each glaze and highlight), so every painting asks the same pace, a parsnip or a portrait: about a click a second on Normal, a little quicker on Hard. Busy paintings get much more light than before, so the hardest Masterpieces leave time to find each colour.
- **Paintings are worth more on harder modes.** A painting's value is set by the mode you painted it on: Simplified half, Relaxed 85%, Normal in full, and Hard three times (it was 1.2x). Hard still gives 20% more XP; the other modes give the same. A Hard, varnished Masterpiece now sells for over 40,000g.
- **Zoom.** From a medium canvas, the mouse wheel zooms the canvas in, up to 3x, towards the mouse, and the reference zooms with it to show the same part of the picture. The arrow keys move round. Thanks to Hiname for asking.
- **Small paintings hang at 1x1.** A framed small painting now takes one tile, like a little picture, in a 1-pixel frame of its style; its picture is cut to what's painted and, if it's bigger than the frame's 14x14 inside, shrunk to fit. Small paintings already hanging are rehung at 1x1 when you load your save. Medium and large paintings are unchanged.
- **The easel shows what your frames do.** At a placed easel, the setup screen shows an icon for each bonus the room's framed paintings give; hover one to see how much.
- **Fixed: subjects missing from the easel.** With a full bag, or many friends, monsters and animals at once, whatever didn't fit on the setup screen was left off, so some villagers couldn't be painted. The items and the portrait row now turn pages (arrows, or the mouse wheel).
- **Fixed: see-through creatures painted too dark.** Ghosts, slimes and other see-through sprites (and see-through items) were matched to pigments by their stored, darkened colours, so a pale ghost came out grey and a slime nearly all Moss. They're now painted in the colours you see in game.
- **Fixed: Sable's shop with Alec Revisited.** Alec's house patch (and its SVE patch) painted over half the shop; the shop now goes onto the forest last. Thanks to AZLeoM for the report.
- **Fixed: an invisible wall beside Sable's shop**, at the tip of the roof's eave.
- **Fixed: the birch frame looked like the white one** in the inventory; every frame's icon now shows its style's mark.
- **Fixed: a black block at the Fair.** Under the painting contest's entries, where an invisible prop sometimes drew black.
- **Fixed: a cut-off portfolio label.** "Ginger Island's beach" ran to three lines under its slot; it's now "Ginger Island's south", and slot labels shrink to fit.
- **The mode is a button beside Begin.** It used to be a line of text under Begin that disappeared whenever a warning showed, so with low paint you couldn't switch to a mode with free paint. Now it's always there, the light line also says how much of your day the painting takes, and only warnings go under the buttons.
- **Winter for the new buildings.** Margot's cottage and Sable's shop are under snow in winter, like the town's own houses: snow on the roofs and sills, the cottage's ivy bare, the shop's planters in for the season.
- **Margot's cottage, tidied.** A cobbled path now runs from the town road to her door, part of the town map once the cottage is built. Her bench by the river is turned to face the water, and the flowerpot that sat on the cottage's corner is gone. Nothing spawns in Sable's fenced yard or against the shop any more.
- **Margot at the festivals.** She goes to all eight once she's in the valley, near Sable, with a line at each. Ask her to the Flower Dance at 4 hearts: she dances in the same white dress as the other dancers.
