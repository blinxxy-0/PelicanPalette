# Pelican Palette 1.3: mastery, animals, and a second romance

Planning notes, 2026-11-01. Nothing here is built yet.

**The goal:** 1.2 made painting reach across the valley. 1.3 gives it an
endgame and more heart:
- **finish the portfolio** that 1.2 started
- **animals and more of the year** as subjects and moments
- **special orders** on the town board
- **Painting Mastery**, with restoration as its reward
- **a second romanceable character**

**Decided so far (2026-11-01):**
- **Mastery:** a sixth pedestal in the Mastery Cave. Claiming Painting Mastery lets you restore damaged old paintings.
- **Special orders:** a few, none of them needing a particular difficulty, so Relaxed players aren't locked out.
- **A second romance** is in 1.3: **Margot Pell**, the conservator from the Zuzu City museum (section 6, idea A; she/her, marriageable by any farmer; the Saloon, then rooms above the museum). Decided 2026-11-01; brief in `docs/conservator_brief.md`.
- **Still no pigments locked behind progression** (the rule from 1.2).

What the 1.2 plan designed but didn't build.

**A sixth page, Faces of the Valley.** Portraits, in groups, at the usual 4 hearts per sitter. A group slot fills with a portrait of any one of its members (decided 2026-11-01):

| Slot | Asks for |
|---|---|
| The Saloon regulars | A portrait of Gus, Emily or Shane |
| A family | A portrait of Robin, Demetrius, Maru or Sebastian |

**The whole book: the gold-leaf frame and the Summit.**
- Finishing every page brings Sable's letter and the **gold-leaf frame**, a one-off frame style, like the Moonlit Frame.
- Then painting the view from the **Summit** plays a short closing scene: Sable joins you at the top and looks through your finished book. It's tender if you're married, warm and proud if not, and it mentions Julian if his story's finished. The game walks it (your rule for cutscenes).
- The Summit view goes into the book as its last page.

**Julian buys a painting a season.** On a visit day, he'll buy one painting of yours at a premium, as an outlet for Masterpieces. He says something about it that depends on how good it is, like his museum review.

## 4. Painting Mastery and restoration

**Claiming it.** 1.6's Mastery Cave has five pedestals, one for each vanilla skill. As far as we know, SpaceCore doesn't let a custom skill add one (SpaceCore 1.28 only refers to the mastery tracker), so we'd add a **sixth pedestal** ourselves: a map edit to the cave and our own claim, spending mastery levels like the vanilla ones, once Painting is level 10. To check before building: how the cave counts claims, and how this looks with other skill mods.

**What it gives:**
- **Restoration.** Damaged old paintings turn up **from early on** (rarely): in the mines, in artifact spots, in fishing treasure. The first one found brings Margot to the valley (section 6). With Painting Mastery you restore them yourself: at an easel you see what's left of the picture, and repaint the damaged cells to match. The better the match, the better the restored painting.
- **Where damaged paintings turn up (decided 2026-11-01).** Each source has its own set of old paintings, so where you find one tells you what it is; together they make the collection Margot builds at the museum:

  | Source | What turns up | How |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Artifact spots** | Old valley paintings: the town and the farms generations ago, rolled in a tin tube | Content Patcher artifact-spot data, a small chance per dig |
  | **Fishing treasure chests** | Paintings from a wreck: seascapes, a ship's portrait, in an oilcloth case | A hook on the treasure chest's contents, a small chance per chest |
  | **The Mines, floors 1 to 120** | Miners' paintings, from crates and barrels: the old mining town, the miners' families | A hook on breakable crates; a little likelier the deeper you go |
  | **The Skull Cavern** | Paintings from the old Calico city: stranger, older, worth more | The same crate hook, and the cavern's treasure rooms |
  | **The Volcano** | One or two Dwarvish paintings, a rare late-game set | The Volcano's chests |
  | **The Travelling Cart** | Now and then, one old painting for sale, from any set | Cart stock, at a high price: a backup for anyone unlucky |

  **The first one is guaranteed,** so Margot arrives within the first season or two of painting: for example the first fishing treasure chest or artifact spot after Painting level 3. (Which, exactly, to decide when building.)
- **Before Mastery (decided 2026-11-01):** Margot restores them for you, for a fee, over a few days, as Clint breaks geodes. Mastery lets you do it yourself, for a better result and more value.
- **Where restored paintings go:** sell them (they're worth more than your own), keep them, or donate them to the museum.
- **The museum's painting collection (decided 2026-11-01).** Donated restorations go into a collection of their own, hung by Margot and Gunther, with its own rewards, like the artifacts and minerals. Each source's set (section 4 table) is one part of the collection. Rewards, a first pass:
  - **The paint-tube Junimo statue,** as furniture: the one from the gallery's 14-heart scene (decided by the user; a must).
  - **A frame style per set:** driftwood (the wreck), iron (the miners), sandstone (Calico), Dwarvish (the Volcano), each at its rarity in section 5.
  - **Gallery furniture:** a velvet rope, a gallery bench, a picture light.
  - **A "living painting":** a framed old painting whose picture moves a little (water, leaves, a lamp flickering).
  - **Margot's loupe,** a trinket (1.6 trinket slot), for the whole collection.
  - **A museum wing:** the restored paintings get a room of their own in the museum once the collection is complete.
- **A mastery item,** like the vanilla ones (for example a master's easel that paints with a little more light).

## 5. What's left over from 1.2

- **Frames (decided 2026-11-01): a look, plus an effect when hung, in many styles, and they can be taken off again.** Framed paintings still have no sale value; a frame is for hanging.
  - **Hung effect (approved 2026-11-01):** a framed painting on a wall inspires paintings made at an easel in the same room, so you build a studio. Each style has one trait; its rarity sets the strength. Only the strongest three in a room count.

    | Trait | At an easel in the same room |
    |---|---|
    | Light | A little more light per painting |
    | Sparks | Inspiration sparks a little more often |
    | Thrift | A small chance a cell's paint isn't used |
    | Learning | A little more Painting XP |
    | Value | Paintings made there are worth a little more |
    | Night | More light, after 7pm only (the Moonlit Frame) |

  - **Styles and rarities (approved 2026-11-01; numbers tuned when building):**

    | Rarity | Styles | From |
    |---|---|---|
    | Common | Pine, oak, birch, painted white | Crafted; Sable's shop |
    | Uncommon | Walnut, red lacquer, rope | Sable's shop, as Painting levels up |
    | Rare | Moonlit, driftwood, iron, sandstone | The Night Market; the restoration sets |
    | Epic | Gold-leaf, Dwarvish | The finished portfolio; the Volcano set |
    | Legendary | Prismatic, Qi's | Qi's gem shop; Qi's special order |

  - **Unframing: the Framer's Bench (approved 2026-11-01).** A crafted machine (a recipe at Painting level 4, or from Sable): put a framed painting in, and the next morning take out the painting and its empty frame. Works with Automate. Frames become something you collect and swap.
  - **The framing screen** needs a picker instead of the Plain and Moonlit buttons: a row of the frames you own, with rarity and trait on hover.
- **Julian's winter outfit,** for his winter visits (eight portraits and his sprites, in PixelLab).
- **"Half as much again"** in Sable's Night Market picker, if anyone finds it confusing.

## 6. The second romance

A romance is about four times the work of Julian: portraits and sprites that match the base game, a home, a schedule for every season, gift tastes, around ten heart events, a wedding, and married life. Whoever it is should tie into 1.3's new things rather than stand apart. Three ideas:

**A. The conservator.** Sent from the Zuzu City museum when old paintings start turning up in the valley, to look after them. They work out of the museum with Gunther, and stay. Ties straight into restoration and Mastery. Knew Sable at the museum, before Sable left (colleagues, not exes).

**B. The gilder.** A framer and gilder who makes Sable's frames (and the gold-leaf one), setting up a small workshop in town. Quiet, precise, good with their hands. Ties into the portfolio's final reward and the frame styles, and gives frames a home in the valley.

**C. The muralist.** A travelling painter who comes to paint a mural on the side of a building in town, over a season, with your help, and stays. Bigger and louder than Sable. Brings the "big project" idea in through the story.

Whoever it is, open for you to decide:
- their gender and pronouns, and who can marry them (Sable is open to every farmer)
- where they live (a new building, a room above the museum, a cottage)
- how they get along with Sable and Julian
